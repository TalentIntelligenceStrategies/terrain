Describe what you are building in a sentence. Terrain shows you who else is in the space and what sits adjacent to it — laid out so you can see which part of it each of them holds.

See how it works See what you get

A founder asks two questions.

Terrain is built to answer them, in that order.

01

Who else is here?

The companies already holding ground around your idea — and which part of it each of them holds. Merged by holder and by patent family, so one invention filed in five countries counts once instead of five times.

02

What is adjacent?

Neighbouring technology you did not know to look for — including whoever holds the ground your approach grew out of, which is the party you would license from or design around. Terrain shows you who is where. It cannot tell you why anyone is absent, and it does not pretend to.

An IP analyst could tell you this.

You cannot hire one for a question you have not asked yet.

What the analysis actually is

A grid. Across the top, the technical approaches to your problem — read out of the patents your sentence found, not out of a list somebody wrote in advance. Down the side, the companies filing against them. Every patent lands in a cell, so you can see in one look who is working on what, rather than reading two hundred results and holding it in your head.

This is not a new kind of analysis and Terrain does not claim it is. It is what an IP professional produces, on a system built for one. What it takes is someone who can name the technical breakdown before the first search runs — which is the thing you came here to find out.

Terrain does that part for you. One sentence, five plain questions, and you approve how it was read. No vocabulary, no query language, nothing to author in advance. The analysis is on screen beside the patents it was drawn from.

  • It shows who is where Not just who is in the space — which part of it each of them holds.
  • It cannot say why anyone is absent An empty cell means that company has not filed there. Whether that is an opening or a dead end is not something the data knows.
  • Nothing runs until you approve it The one place a misreading is cheap to fix is before the search, and that is where Terrain puts it.

Everything over one search.

Six views, on one screen, beside the patents they are drawn from. The map leads.

The map

Technology × holder

Which approaches the space is built on, and which company holds each part of it. Tone is how many; a hatch marks the cells where filings are climbing.

Rivals

Who else is here

Named holders near your idea, what they hold in scope, and when they last filed. One that stopped filing years ago is a different fact from one filing now.

Filings over time

How the space is moving

Whether the space is early or late, said in words. The most recent stretch is shaded and left out: a patent publishes about eighteen months after it is filed, so that window is not a decline.

Where it is filed

Which markets

How the filings split across jurisdictions, and what share of them is in yours. A space that is crowded somewhere else is a different problem from one crowded at home.

Live and expired

What is still enforceable

How much of the crowd is still in force. An expired patent is not an obstacle — it is prior art, and the two are opposite situations for you.

Lineage

What this grew out of

The most-cited filings the rest of the space is built on. If you end up licensing from anyone, or designing around anyone, it is most likely one of these.

Built for a founder, not an analyst.

Some of what is missing here is missing on purpose.

  • No boolean search boxWriting a good patent query takes the vocabulary you came here to get. Terrain builds the query from your sentence and keeps it out of sight.
  • No report to downloadTerrain is software. You log in and search; nothing is produced to order, and there is no document to wait for.
  • No chart pickerA shelf of chart types is a shelf of decisions you have no basis to make yet. Six views, chosen for you, in one order.
  • No verdict on any patentOpen one and you get its record as published — claims and all. Results are ranked, and near the top means close to your words: an ordering is not a verdict. What you do not get is Terrain reading a patent for you. Whether one affects you is a legal question, and it is your attorney's call.
