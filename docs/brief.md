# TIS Terrain — Brief

> Source of truth for what Terrain is. Settled 2026-08-31 unless marked open.
> Add to this file before creating a new one.

## 1 · The product

**Terrain is patent search with strategic direction, for founders working out where their idea
sits.**

The user arrives with a concept. Whether they have filed, are mid-filing, or have no intention of
filing does not change the job — early-stage IP positions are all over the place, and Terrain
should not assume one. Terrain answers two questions:

1. **Who else is here?** The competitors already holding ground in this space.
2. **What is adjacent?** Similar and neighbouring technology they didn't know to look for.

Self-serve software, subscription-priced, English-native, searching the full Innovue patent
database.

***Amended 2026-09-10 — there were three questions until today, and the third is dropped.*** *Where
are the gaps?* — what nobody has claimed, and what that implies about where to build — came off with
the adoption of Innovue's architecture at [platform.md](platform.md) §1a. The map's rows are now
**holders**, and a Technology × Assignee matrix cannot carry the claim: an empty cell on it means
*this holder has not filed against this approach*, which is a competitor's profile and not open
ground. That rule was already written down — `platform.md` §6.1b forbade the word `Open` on the
holder axis while there were two axes to switch between. Making that axis the only one made the
prohibition the whole of the widget rather than half of it.

**And the sentence that closed the paragraph above is retracted with it.** It read: *“The third
question is the one that makes it a product rather than a search box: search tools return results,
Terrain returns a direction.”* The second clause survives as copy; the first does not. Nothing in
this folder is to be written as though **rendering absence** is what Terrain sells.

**What makes it a product rather than a search box, restated — 2026-09-10.** **Legibility and
self-serve.** The same engine, operable by the person who actually has the question. A founder
cannot run IPtech: it wants a taxonomy authored before the first search is typed (`platform.md`
§0), it is built in the vocabulary of the profession, and it assumes an analyst on the other side of
it. Terrain is one sentence, five questions and a map — no analyst, no 魚骨, no definitions table, and
no term of art anywhere on the surface.

**This is a durable gap in a way a chart feature is not**, and that is the reason it replaces the
one it replaces. *We show absence* was a claim about what Terrain draws, and a claim about what a
product draws is beaten the day somebody draws it — §3's amendment records exactly that happening to
the novelty claim in `platform.md` §0. *A founder can operate this and cannot operate that* is a
claim about **who can use the thing**, which does not fall to a feature. [case.md](case.md) §3 has
made this argument since 2026-09-01 as a supporting one. It is now the argument.

***And the marketing core statement is retired with it, recorded here rather than only in the
view.*** [marketing.md](marketing.md) §1 held one sentence as the thing the whole product page hung
on: *“A founder searching patents cannot tell ‘nobody is here’ from ‘I asked the wrong question.’
Both come back almost empty.”* It was a good sentence and it is **a claim about absence**, so it
goes. So does the scaffold built on it — *an index versus a map* — and the protected value carrier,
*a gap is an absence, and absences cannot be enumerated.*

**It is retired here because retiring it is a positioning decision, not a copy decision.** A view
may not drop the thing its own source says the product is for; the source has to drop it first.
`marketing.md` §1 is rewritten against this paragraph, and the replacement core statement is a
legibility claim rather than an absence one.
**Terrain is software, not a report.** The user logs in and searches. Nothing is produced to order
by a human, nothing is delivered as a document. Never write copy that describes Terrain in report
language.

*This lock is unchanged, and one live discussion runs up against it.* On 2026-09-04 the question
**what does the founder actually walk away with?** was asked and found unanswered anywhere in these
documents. It is open, and the thinking is recorded at [platform.md](platform.md) §7a.8 — **a
discussion, not a proposal.** Two things are worth carrying here rather than leaving buried in it:

- **The test that keeps any answer honest is already written**, at `platform.md` §7a.4: *a title
  page, a download, or the word* report *is the line.* A page with a URL is software; selectable
  text is not a document; a PDF is.
- **A downloadable file was put to the decision on 2026-09-04 and declined**, in favour of a link
  plus copyable text. That closes the strongest threat to this paragraph. If a later session
  proposes a PDF, a slide or a cover page, **that is an amendment to this lock and must be argued as
  one.**

**Language:** English only. The parent TIS surfaces are bilingual; Terrain deliberately is not.
No Chinese copy, no bilingual markup, no CJK typeface. Decided 2026-08-31.

**ICP:** early-stage startups and their founders. Not IP departments, not attorneys, not analysts.
The user does not know patent vocabulary and should never need to learn it to get value.

**User and buyer are not the same, and only the user constrains the design.** Added 2026-09-01 while
writing [case.md](case.md). The market is **US** founders, and frequently someone else pays — the
university, accelerator, incubator or investor backing them. Institutions are a **channel**, not a
second ICP: they change how seats are sold, never who the interface is designed for. A university
tech transfer office is closer to the excluded category above than to the included one, so the lock
in the paragraph above holds unchanged — the person inside the product is a founder who does not
speak patent. Do not let an institutional buyer justify an analyst-facing feature.

## 2 · The name

**`TIS Terrain`** — the working name. Not locked. Everything in this folder is written as though it
holds, because the alternative is writing nothing, but it is a decision that can still move.

It is positioned as a TIS-endorsed sub-brand, not a standalone brand and not a descriptive label.

*Why Terrain.* It maps directly onto the job — competitors, adjacent technology, who is standing
where. It verbs naturally ("map the terrain", "your patent terrain"). It is ownable in a way that a
descriptive name is not. And the map register is an extensible namespace rather than a single word:
Bearing, Waypoint, Atlas, Contour are all available for later products or modules without a
rebrand.

*Why Terrain, second and stronger reason — re-based 2026-09-10.* Discovered during the IPtech audit
on 2026-08-31: the engine's own analysis vocabulary is already territorial. Its fourth analysis
family is **技術脈絡** — technology lineage, literally a *pathway*; its competitive family is
**競合分析**, contending over the same ground; and the system it all sits in is 專利布局分析系統, a
patent **layout** analysis system, layout in the sense of how ground is laid out and held. Terrain
is therefore not a metaphor imposed on the capability; it is the capability's native vocabulary
rendered into English a founder reads without instruction. Innovue built the map and described it in
Chinese to analysts. Terrain names it in English for the person standing on it.

*The audit also found the matrix dividing its results into three named zones — 地雷區 (minefield),
新興區 (emerging ground) and 處女地帶 (**virgin territory**) — and until 2026-09-10 the paragraph
above led on those, with the third carrying most of the weight.* **Terrain does not render those
zones, and this name does not depend on them.** The trio is quoted here as an audit finding about
Innovue's vocabulary, in the past tense, and nowhere else: `platform.md` §6.1 and
[design-language.md](design-language.md) §3.4 dropped all three on 2026-09-10, and §1 dropped the
question they answered. **A later session that finds 處女地帶 in this paragraph must not restore it to
§6.1.**

***Why the re-basing costs nothing, which is the useful half of this amendment.*** The argument's
truth condition was always a fact about *their* vocabulary rather than about *our* features, so
losing a feature does not touch it — 技術脈絡 is untouched by every change made on 2026-09-10, and
Lineage (`platform.md` §6.4) still ships.

**And §1's two surviving questions are both spatial, which is a better argument than the one
dropped.** *Who else is **in the space**?* *What is **adjacent** to mine?* Occupants and neighbours
are what a terrain has. The dropped question was about a terrain being *empty*, which was never the
part of the word doing the work — and the Technology × Assignee matrix renders occupancy and
adjacency directly, which is what §1 now claims.

**So the name stands on three legs and none of them is the dropped pillar:** the job maps onto it,
their own register is territorial, and the map namespace extends — Bearing, Waypoint, Atlas, Contour,
none of which depended on emptiness either. The name is still not locked and clearance is still
outstanding; this re-founds it, it does not settle it.

*Why endorsed rather than standalone.* Equity compounds into TIS, the SABCD rating engine's
credibility transfers for free, and it is far cheaper to launch. Revisit only if Terrain is ever
meant to be financed or exited on its own.

*Why not "Plus".* A filler name like "IPtech Plus" was considered and rejected on three commercial
grounds, not taste: it permanently frames TIS as an upgrade SKU of someone else's system, so
pricing anchors to their list; "IP tech" is generic English and unownable as a trademark; and it
inherits IPTECH's identity as a Chinese-language enterprise system, which is precisely the
association an English-native founder-facing product needs to shed.

*Live risk.* "Terrain" is a real English word used by outdoor, mapping, and some B2B software
brands. The `TIS Terrain` lockup is defensible where bare "Terrain" likely is not — another reason
the endorsed architecture is right. **An actual USPTO / TIPO and domain clearance check is
outstanding and must happen before anyone designs a logo.**

## 3 · Innovue

Terrain is built on the capability behind Innovue's IPtech, and Innovue is a TIS shareholder and
the co-developer of the SABCD engine. The relationship is **attribution only**:

- "Powered by Innovue" as an attribution line.
- The patent database cited in body copy where it does credibility work.
- **Innovue never appears in the product name or the primary lockup.**

Reasons: a name-level dependency caps pricing against their list, blocks the trademark, and forces
a rebrand if the engine ever changes. The parent monorepo owns the approved attribution phrasing in
`visual-guide.md` — check it there before inventing wording.

**One recorded exception, 2026-09-07: Innovue's mark appears in the masthead of an outbound
document.** `design/previews/iptech-feature-request.html` — the one-page feature request sent *to*
Innovue — pairs the TIS submark with `Innovue_Logo_Blue_eng.svg` across a hairline divider, in
Innovue's own brand blue.

**This does not amend the rule above; it scopes it.** The attribution-only rule governs the
**product** — Terrain's name, its primary lockup, and any surface a founder sees. A document
*addressed to* Innovue, whose entire subject is which of their features we intend to build on, is
correspondence between two companies, and correspondence carries both marks. No founder sees this
page, and nothing in the product changed.

Two limits hold it there. The pairing is **only** valid on outbound documents to Innovue — a page
addressed to anyone else keeps attribution-only. And Innovue's blue is permitted **because it is
their mark, not our accent**: `design-language.md` §2 governs colour that carries information in a
Terrain interface, and Terrain still has no accent decision. If a future outbound document wants the
pairing, reuse the masthead from that file rather than re-deciding it.

**Amended 2026-09-08 — the attribution line in the product renders their mark, in their blue.** The
sentence above used to say *"and every product surface"* among the places that keep attribution-only.
The dashboard, the set and the widget page now carry *Powered by* in italic followed by
`Innovue_Logo_Blue_eng_inline.svg`, and the conversation surface's text-only line was upgraded to
match. This is a real widening of the 2026-09-07 exception and not a clarification of it.

*Three of those four surfaces no longer exist — §4's adoption entry. The line survives the collapse
unchanged and unmoved: it sits at the foot of the working screen and at the foot of the
conversation, which is every surface there is. Nothing about the exception was re-argued, and
nothing needed to be.*

**What the rule was actually protecting, and what it still protects.** The three bullets at the top
of this section are about **naming and the lockup**, and every one of them is untouched: Innovue is
not in the product name, not in the primary lockup, and the TIS submark is still the only mark in
the chrome — the sidebar until 2026-09-10, the masthead since. What changed is that the *attribution*, which was always permitted, is now rendered as
a mark instead of as four words. The commercial reasons for the rule — a name-level dependency caps
pricing against their list, blocks the trademark, and forces a rebrand if the engine ever changes —
none of them attach to a credit line at the foot of a page.

**The colour is theirs, and that is the whole argument.** `#006CB6` is a fourth thing on a surface
where `design-language.md` §2 permits colour in exactly three, and it is admissible on the same
ground the masthead exception uses: it is a third party's mark reproduced as issued, not a hue
Terrain has chosen for itself. **Terrain still has no accent**, primary actions are still near-black
in light and near-white in dark, and nothing else on any surface gained a colour. The test that keeps
this honest is that the blue may only ever appear *inside the Innovue mark itself* — the moment it
turns up in a border, a chip, a chart or a button, this exception has been misread.

*Measured, because the mark renders on two grounds:* their blue is **5.50** on the light card and
**3.35** on the dark. Both are legal — a logotype is exempt from the text-contrast minimum and 3.35
clears the 3:1 for non-text content — but 3.35 is quiet, and a partner's mark reproduced quietly is a
worse outcome than the rule was protecting against.

**So the line is theme-aware: their primary in light, their own white mark in dark**, at 17.65. The
point is that *both files are theirs* — `Innovue_Logo_Blue_eng_inline.svg` and
`Innovue_Logo_Light_eng_inline.svg`, identical `viewBox`, so the swap is pixel-stable. The
alternative would have been a Terrain-authored light variant of their mark, which is us redrawing
someone else's brand to suit our background, and that is the thing this section exists to prevent.
Two of the eight files are therefore tracked rather than one.

**And it costs a redistribution decision, which is recorded rather than assumed.**
`brand/logos/innovue/` is gitignored as *"a third party's trademark files. Cited in the documents,
not redistributed"*, and a published page cannot reference a file that is not published. Exactly one
of the eight — the tight-cropped inline variant the line uses — is now tracked; the Chinese lockups
and the unused colourways stay out. The negation needs `brand/logos/innovue/*` rather than the bare
directory, because git will not descend into an excluded directory and a `!` line under one silently
does nothing.

### What Innovue's IPtech actually is (competitive read, 2026-08-31)

From `innovue.ltd/products-iptech/`: IPtech is `專利布局分析系統` — a patent layout/portfolio
analysis system. It sits beside `WEBPAT 專利檢索資料庫` (a search database) in a rack of
management systems (專利 / 商標 / 合約 / 營業秘密) plus TIPS consulting. Chinese-only, no English
product marketing, and the product page itself is essentially one large image.

**Amended 2026-09-03, from inside the platform.** The paragraph above describes the *marketing
site*, and it stands. **The product is not Chinese-only** — there is an English UI, and TIS has an
account. What is incomplete is the English, not its absence: a Chinese column header sits in an
otherwise English table, applicant names render untranslated, and the vocabulary is non-idiomatic
throughout (*Citings*, *Overdue*, *Tech-Effx*). Evidence and screens are indexed in
`visual-reference/iptech-screenshots-identified/README.md` §2.2.

The platform's own structure, which replaces what we had inferred, is its top navigation:
**Search · Fishbone · View · Hierarchy · M-Map · T-Map · Report · Project.** `T-Map › Tech-Effx` is
the technology-function matrix; `Fishbone` is a top-level destination, which is §0's finding made
literal; `M-Map` is the analysis engine.

*Two corrections, 2026-09-04, both from walking the platform rather than reading about it.* The
engine is **60 entries across eleven dimension groups**, not the "11 × ~11" this sentence once
claimed — and `T-Map` adds eight more, so the menus walk to 128 entries but only **68 distinct
destinations**, because a shared entry renders identically under either map. Innovue's marketing says
"126 charts". And **`Hierarchy` is no longer uncatalogued**: it is the classification workbench where
an analyst builds the taxonomy and assigns patents to it by hand, which makes it the single clearest
statement of what Terrain automates. Both are recorded in [platform.md](platform.md) §8, which is now
the canonical mapping.

That is a Taiwanese enterprise IP toolchain: all-caps utility codes bolted to functional
descriptors, sold to in-house legal and IP departments who already speak the language. **It is a
tool.** Terrain is a decision product for founders who don't. Different buyer, different job,
different naming grammar — that gap is the differentiation, and it is why Terrain must not sound
like a tier of IPtech.

**Amended 2026-09-08 — Innovue is building a semantic search surface, and it is aimed much closer to
our person than the platform is.** `快檢通 · AI 快速檢索` is a separate product of theirs, seen for the
first time on 2026-09-08 with a TIS account signed in. One field on the screen, no filter rail, a
sentence in — and a technology structure generated from that sentence with the patents under each
node. It has an English interface, and the suggestions it generates are translated too. Nine captures
and the full reading are in `visual-reference/iptech-semantic-search/README.md`; the capability rows
are `platform.md` §8.7, and it forced a correction to `platform.md` §0.

**The paragraph above still describes the platform, and it no longer describes everything Innovue
ships.** *It is a tool* was written about `分類通` and `T-Map` — an analyst's workbench sold to people
who already speak the language — and that reading holds. It was never a claim that Innovue could not
build something else, and it should not be read as one now that they have.

**What the differentiation rests on, restated against the thing that actually exists.** Not that
nobody else can turn a sentence into a structure — they demonstrably can, and §0's old novelty claim
was corrected rather than defended. What that surface does not do is **decide anything**: it returns a
ranked list with a similarity score printed on every row, and a diagram of the technology, and leaves
the founder to draw the conclusion. There is no gate, no narrowing round, and no map of who holds
which ground. **Terrain is the layer that turns a good retrieval engine into an answer**, and that
gap is narrower and more defensible than the one this section used to claim.

*The third clause read “no map of where the ground is taken and where it is open” until 2026-09-10.
The map's rows are holders now and it does not claim openness — §1. The rest of the sentence is
untouched, and the score clause reads differently in light of §4: their surface printing a score is
no longer something Terrain undoes.*

*Whether Terrain sits on that surface or beside it is a working direction and not a decision. §4
holds it open.*

## 4 · Locked, open, and from scratch

**Locked**

- Innovue as attribution only, never in the name. *Since 2026-09-08 the attribution is rendered as
  their mark rather than as four words, and the naming half of this line is unchanged — §3 carries
  the amendment and the two limits that still hold it.*
- The endorsed sub-brand architecture — the TIS mark appears in the lockup.
- Two typefaces: **Urbanist** (text and display), **Inconsolata** (numerals and figures).
- **English only** — no Chinese surface, no bilingual markup.
- The product definition and ICP in §1.
- **Terrain renders the record, not an opinion about the record.** Locked 2026-09-09. A patent's
  identifiers, abstract and claims are the public text of a granted patent and Terrain shows them;
  every *reading* of that text stays out — no plain-English decode, no match highlighting, no
  *strong match*. This replaced a rule that said Terrain must not show a claim at
  all, which conflated showing the evidence with delivering the verdict and, being undefined, could
  not be applied to anything. **It is positioning and not a feature decision**: it is the sentence
  that separates Terrain from a patent-analysis tool for a founder who would not be able to tell the
  difference from a screenshot. [platform.md](platform.md) §7a.3 carries the argument and the table
  of what falls on each side.

  ***Amended 2026-09-10 — the relevance score is struck from that list, and the prohibition on it is
  removed outright with no successor rule.*** The list above read *“no plain-English decode, no match
  highlighting, ~~no relevance score~~, no strong match”* until today. Terrain prints the score
  Innovue's surface returns.

  **This is a real weakening and it is recorded as one.** It is admitted here rather than in
  `platform.md` because the sentence it changes is *positioning*, by this entry's own words — and a
  positioning decision is this file's to make. Three things narrow it, and none of them undoes it:

  - **The rest of the lock is untouched.** Decode, highlighting and *strong match* are still out, and
    the reason still holds: those are Terrain **reading** a patent for a founder. A score is the
    engine's own ordering, printed rather than paraphrased.
  - **The line moved, from *is it an opinion* to *is it ours*.** That is a narrower line and a
    weaker one, and pretending otherwise would be the failure this file exists to prevent. What it
    still refuses is Terrain **generating** a verdict; what it now admits is Terrain **not hiding**
    one the engine already produced.
  - **The honest reason it moved is adoption, not argument.** `platform.md` §1a's flow prints
    `分數 0.8955` on every row and Terrain is adopting that flow. Deleting the number would have been
    a deletion we performed on somebody else's shipped surface every time it returned one, and
    `platform.md` §4b had already recorded that honouring the old rule *“becomes an act of deletion
    rather than an omission”*. The old rule's own words are what settled it.

  **What does not change**, and it is the part a later session will be tempted to relax next: no
  ranking language may read as a verdict. *Ranked by relevance* is an ordering; *strong match*,
  *best fit* and *most relevant* are readings, and they stay out whatever number is beside them.
- ~~**The dashboard is two pages — `Market` and `Technology` — behind one toggle.**~~ **Retired
  2026-09-10, one day after it was decided, by the entry below.** There is no dashboard to split:
  the widgets and the result list share one screen, and the widgets stack in one right pane. The
  argument the entry made — that four widgets were answering two questions and the founder was
  sorting it out for themselves — is not wrong and is not solved here; it is **carried forward as
  the ordering of that one pane**, map first, and recorded at `platform.md` §6.5 rather than
  deleted. The observation that IPtech's own `M-Map` opens on the filings trend survives with §6.3.

  ***One finding in the retired entry outlives the toggle and is kept here rather than with it.***
  The split landed on **a real seam in the engine**: `Market` is computable from bibliographic
  fields, `Technology` needs a taxonomy generated from the founder's sentence, and the two halves
  have almost disjoint API surfaces. That is a fact about what we would be asking Innovue for, it is
  what made the split worth having rather than cosmetic, and it is still true of the widgets now
  that they share a pane. It bears on cost and on §10, not on layout.
  *And it was never a claim about what `M-Map` and `T-Map` mean* — sixty of IPtech's sixty-eight
  destinations sit under both maps, so the two are not a market/technology split in their product.
  `platform.md` §8.3 carries that evidence, and §8.3 now also carries the consequence it predicted:
  the map's source moved to `Tech-Effx › Company`, a T-Map-only destination.

- **The `Market` / `Technology` toggle is back — restored 2026-09-10, the same evening it was
  retired.** Locked again. The entry above struck it and the reasoning in that entry is still
  correct as far as it goes; **what it got wrong is the inference.** *There is no dashboard* is a
  reason not to have a dashboard. It is not a reason for six views to stack in one scrolling
  column, and re-reading it that way was the error.

  **What is locked is the split, and it is IPtech's rather than ours.** `Technology` is the map,
  because `Tech-Effx › Company` is the only destination reachable under `T-Map` and not under
  `M-Map`. `Market` is the other five, and it lands. **This makes `Technology` a one-view page**,
  which is faithful to the source and looks thin — accepted rather than fixed, because the
  alternative is inventing a split that is not theirs, which is the thing §8.3 warns against doing
  to somebody else's menu.

  ***This is the third state this decision has been in inside one file, and the churn is the
  record.*** Locked, retired, locked. It is written as three entries rather than by editing the
  first two away, because a reader holding a citation to any of them should find out what happened
  to it. `platform.md` §6.5 carries the full sequence, the split table and the one thing in it most
  likely to be wrong — that with `Market` landing, the map is not on the founder's first screen.

- **Every patent the search returns feeds every view.** Decided 2026-09-10. There is no per-row
  selection in the result list: the checkbox, the recommended cut and *Select the top N* are
  deleted, and the corpus is set by the gate and by the fifth narrowing question alone. **Sort and
  filter replace them** — both change what the founder is *looking at*, neither changes what is
  counted.

  **This is positioning and not only a feature decision**, which is why it is here. A permanently
  visible list of patents with checkboxes that redraw a chart is a **curation workbench**, and
  `platform.md` §3 admitted the checkboxes against three requirements precisely because it knew
  that. Removing them is that amendment being reopened and answered rather than a new idea, and it
  is the clearest statement so far of the line in §1: Terrain is the analysis a founder can operate,
  not the tool an analyst operates. `platform.md` §6a.2 carries the argument and the cost.

- **There is no chat surface.** Decided 2026-09-10. The masthead control and the panel behind it are
  deleted, along with the landscape summary that was only reachable from them. Terrain opens on a
  conversation and that is the only one; a second conversational surface reached from the chrome
  was the same affordance offered twice. **The gate survives and is now the only correction point
  *and* the only entrance to one** — `platform.md` ~~§5~~ records what that costs, which is that a
  founder on a built map cannot narrow the corpus without starting again.

- **Innovue's architecture is adopted — one working screen, and the map is Technology × Assignee.**
  Decided 2026-09-10, superseding the shape this file has described since 2026-08-31.
  [platform.md](platform.md) §1a is the adoption record and carries what was proposed, what each of
  its five conflicts cost, and how each was resolved. Four things in it are positioning rather than
  build, which is why they are here:

  - **Three surfaces, not five, and only two on the path.** The conversation, and then one working
    screen: the ranked patents on the left, the views on the right, the patent record over the
    views. The set, the dashboard and the widget page are gone as *surfaces*; what each of them did
    that mattered is on the working screen. **The points page survives** as what it always was — a
    destination reached from the chrome, on nobody's path to an answer.
  - **The gate stays**, and it is the one conflict that was resolved **against** the proposal.
    §4's confirm step is now the only correction point anywhere in the product — with a single fixed
    axis scheme there is no axis veto to fall back on — so it is load-bearing in a way it has never
    been. `platform.md` §4 says so in those terms.
  - **The map is one view: technology across the columns, holders down the rows.** No switch, no
    generated outcome axis, no founder veto over either list. This is what §1's amendment follows
    from, and the two are one decision seen from two sides.
  - **A top masthead, not a 260px sidebar.** Arithmetic, not taste: the map needs 944px and the
    result list stops being readable under 340px, which does not fit a 1440px laptop beside a
    260px rail. `platform.md` §1a has the sum. **This is the change most likely to lose something
    quietly** — the rail carried the theme control, the project list, version history and the points
    figure, and `design-language.md` protected the theme control by name from exactly this kind of
    disappearance. Anything that does not survive the move is to be recorded as a loss rather than
    absorbed.

- **Colour carries information, or it is not there.** Decided 2026-08-31, superseding the
  monochrome-wireframe rule. Colour appears only on discrete states, delta pills, and the three
  chart layers — and it encodes direction, never desirability. There is still no accent, so primary

**Open — needs a decision**

- **The name.** `TIS Terrain` is the working name, used throughout as if settled. It is not.
  USPTO / TIPO and domain clearance is outstanding and gates any logo work.
- **A Chinese product name, for the website's nav only.** Opened 2026-09-08 by the marketing page.

  navigation label on a different surface.
- **The accent.** None chosen; primary actions are near-black in the meantime. Listed here because
  [design-language.md](design-language.md) §2, §3.1 and §11 all cite *this list* as the place the
  accent is held open, and until 2026-09-04 it was not actually on it. It is upstream-affecting —
  see §6.

  **It has a candidate, a candidate is not a decision, and the candidate was tried in production for
  one day.** `#C2F662`, sampled from `visual-reference/visual-inspiration/Screenshot 2026-08-31 at 2.13.59 PM.png`, shipped
  2026-09-04 as `--mark-1`, the chart hue in `design-language.md` §3.7 — and **withdrawn 2026-09-05.
  There is now no hue anywhere in Terrain.** The marks are neutral ramp steps and a hatch; §2 of
  `design-language.md` carries the amendment. It was also darkened to `#9ACB2F` and reverted inside
  the shipped day — the drop bought contrast and cost the glow.

  **The withdrawal is the most useful thing this entry now records**, and it is a finding about *how*
  to trial an accent rather than about the colour. Shipping it as a chart mark looked like the safe,
  reversible way to see the hue in context. It was not: it went out in the one artifact addressed to
  Innovue and to TIS management, where **a reviewer does not distinguish a chart mark from an
  accent on sight** — so a candidate we were not committed to was about to be reviewed as the
  decision. `CLAUDE.md` predicts exactly this in its reason for holding the accent open: *a
  placeholder accent becomes the thing everyone reviews instead of the structure.* It also put the
  prototype in conflict with `case.md` §6.4, which tells that reader there is **no accent colour**.
  **Whenever the accent is decided, it gets decided deliberately and recorded here first — not
  trialled inside a deliverable.**

  The numbers below stand and are the evidence any future proposal argues from. Three things
  recommend it and one warns against it, all measured:

  - It clears every reserved status hue by a wide margin — **31.7° from `--state-live`**, 49.9° from
    pending, 97.1° from expired — so it cannot be read as *good* or *expired*.
  - Against near-black it measures **12.16:1**, which is what a filled primary button needs. The
    reference screen uses it as exactly that.
  - All three screens the aesthetic was sampled from are **one warm accent plus ink**. None uses a
    categorical palette. If Terrain's look is derived from them, an accent is the shape of the thing
    it is derived from.
  - Against it: at **1.26:1 on white** it cannot carry a thin mark, a hairline or text unaided. As a
    button fill that is fine; as a general-purpose accent it constrains every use to a dark partner.

  **Still deliberately not decided, and now with the evidence of having half-decided it once.**
  Adopting it would amend `CLAUDE.md`, this list, and `design-language.md` §2, §3.1 and §11
  together — *all four in one deliberate pass*, which is precisely what shipping it as a chart mark
  routed around. If a later session proposes it, that is the change to argue, the numbers above are
  the evidence to argue it from, and the paragraph above is the reason not to trial it by putting it
  on screen first.

  **2026-09-08 — the direction is green, and the direction is still not the decision.** Stated in
  session: *"I'm going to use green for Terrain. I just haven't gotten there yet."* Recorded here
  rather than acted on, because that is what the paragraph above requires. Two consequences worth
  having in writing:

  - **It narrows the candidate rather than settling it.** `#C2F662` is itself a green — a
    yellow-green at 1.26:1 on white. The marketing gradient added the same day
    (`brand/assets/imagery/terrain/terrain-forest.*`) is a *forest* green, dark and saturated, and
    the two are nowhere near each other. So "green" does not yet pick a value, and a future proposal
    has to say **which** green and against which partner.
  - **There are two accent systems, and only one of them is this entry.** The TIS website gives
    every surface one accent — `--surface-accent-licensing` #EC4200, `--surface-accent-signal`
    #0EA5E9 — used on the product page's hero clause and rationed under 15% of the surface.
    Terrain's marketing page needs `--surface-accent-terrain` in that slot. **That is a
    marketing-surface token, authored upstream in `brand/design-tokens.md`, and it is not the same
    decision as the product accent this entry holds open.** The green nav gradient sits in the
    website's system, which §6 already anticipates as *gradient↔pillar pairings*. It is not a hue
    entering Terrain's product palette, and the product interface stays hueless until this entry
    says otherwise. Keeping the two apart is the whole reason this is written down: a later session
    reading a green marketing page must not conclude the product accent was decided.

- **Terrain's relationship to Innovue's semantic search surface.** Opened 2026-09-08 by
