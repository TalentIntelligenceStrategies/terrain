Common questions

Your search and your map

How long does a build take?
A few minutes. You can close Terrain and come back — the map is waiting when you return, because a build belongs to the project rather than to the tab you started it in.
Can Terrain tell me whether I am free to file?
No. Terrain shows you what has already been filed and which company holds it. Whether any of it stands in your way is a legal question, and that one is for a patent attorney.
Why are the rows on the map companies?
Because that is the question a founder can act on. Each row is a holder and each column an approach, so a row tells you what one company has built and a column tells you who is working the same way you are. An empty cell means that company has not filed there — it is a fact about them, not about the technology.
Where does the patent data come from?
The Innovue database — around 180 million records, covering the major offices. Terrain searches it and lays out what it returns; it does not hold a database of its own.
Can I change what a map covers after it is built?
Not from the map. You can go back to an earlier version of the project, or start a new search. Changing what gets searched costs a fresh run across the whole database, so it goes through the same approval step the first search did.
Does sorting or filtering the list change the map?
No. Every patent the search returned feeds the views, and it keeps doing so. Sorting and filtering change what you are looking at, not what the views count.

Points and your plan

When do points come back?
At the start of each quarter. Points you have not spent do not carry over, and a build is charged when you approve it rather than when the map finishes drawing.
What spends points?
Building a map. Reading it does not — opening a record, sorting the list, re-ranking it, switching views and reverting to an earlier version are all free, however many times you do them.
What happens if I run out?
Everything you have already built stays open and stays readable. You cannot start a new build until the next quarter or until you move up a plan.

Your projects

Who can see my projects?
Only you. There are no shared workspaces and no seats in Terrain — an account is one person's.
Can I send a map to someone else?
Not from inside Terrain today. Nothing stops you showing it on your own screen, and telling us you need to send one is useful — it is what decides whether this gets built.
What happens to my projects if I close my account?
They go, along with the searches and the maps built from them. Nothing is recoverable afterwards, so take what you need out first.
